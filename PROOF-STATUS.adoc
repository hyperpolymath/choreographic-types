== Fragment formalised (scope, stated before the claims)

Finite graded global types with message and *binary* choice; endpoint
projection with *plain merge* (a non-participant's two branches must
project identically); cuts as frontiers (`stop` / `cont-*`); consistent
cuts = plain merge still holds on the truncated branches. Grades are drawn
from any `ResourceAlgebra`. *Not* in the fragment: recursion (μ-types),
full merge, delegation, n-ary choice (a fold of binary), queues as data,
and data-dependent warrants (the frame carries only what the type
determines). Both components of K-CUT-WARRANT — bound and transport — are
in, for this fragment. Asynchrony, with messages and selections in flight,
is a layer over the same fragment (section "Asynchrony" below).

== Asynchrony (Async.agda, AsyncKnowledge.agda, AsyncBound.agda)

Ladder increment I3a, pre-registered before any proof in
link:docs/pre-registration-addendum-2026-10-19.adoc[]. A cut is still a
prefix of the global type's tree, but each message and each selection in it
is delivered or in flight, and the receiver of anything in flight does
nothing later in the prefix (`Causal`). The sender pays an interaction's
grade at send, the receiver at receipt (`grade↾ₐ`). Worlds are causal cuts
consistent for every party; accessibility is sameness of view, as in W-1.
All eight claims came out as pre-registered.

== Echo-graded projection commuting square (Base/EchoProjection.agda)

The choreographic analog of echo-types' `choreo-grade-commute`.  echo-types'
`RoleGEcho` is a 2D family indexed by Role and Grade: at keep grade it
carries `RoleEcho r true` (a type that genuinely varies with the role); at
residue and forget grades it collapses to `⊤`.  `choreo-grade-commute` says
role transport and grade degradation commute — 18 cases, all `refl`, with
one non-trivial case (`c⊑s, keep≤keep`) where both sides compute to
`client-to-server e`, a function between different types.

The choreographic analog defines two 2D families indexed by Party and
EGrade: one carrying the restricted grade (grade-indexed), the other the
projected local type (local-indexed), both collapsing to `⊤` at residue
and forget grades.  The party transport requires the restricted grades
(grade version) or projections (local version) to agree.

[cols="1,3,1",options="header"]
|===
| ID | Claim | Status
| EP-1 | `echo-grade-commute`: grade degradation and party transport commute on the grade-indexed 2D family `CGEchoGrade`, for any parties with the same restricted grade.  Closes by `refl` in all six cases | PROVEN (`EchoProjection.echo-grade-commute`)
| EP-2 | `echo-local-commute`: grade degradation and party transport commute on the local-type-indexed 2D family `CGEchoLocal`, for any parties with the same projection.  Closes by `refl` in all six cases | PROVEN (`EchoProjection.echo-local-commute`)
| EP-3 | Two-event witnesses for `msg 0 1 g₁ (msg 2 3 g₂ end)`: each sender/receiver pair has equal restricted grades; cross-event parties 0 and 2 also agree when `g₁ ≡ g₂`, and participant 0 agrees with outsider 4 when `g₁ ≡ 𝟙`. Outsiders 4 and 5 have equal projections; parties 0 and 1 do not. These witnesses are not an exhaustive classification of transport | PROVEN (`TwoEvent.same-grade-01`, `same-grade-23`, `same-grade-02`, `same-grade-04`, `same-proj-45`, `proj-0≢proj-1`)
|===

What EP-3 says about the keystone: echo-types' commuting square has a
non-trivial case because `RoleEcho Client true` and `RoleEcho Server true`
are different types, and `client-to-server` is a genuine function between
them. The choreographic projection `proj p G` is a value of the fixed type
`Local`, and `grade↾ p G` is a value of the fixed type `Grade`. The
implemented transport preserves that value and changes its equality
witness; it requires equality of the two parties' grades or projections.
Its square commutes by reduction and does not construct a role-specific
conversion between different projections. This does not rule out other
maps between the singleton families. The B-5 open item (role transport)
remains open: these equality-based squares do not establish its
transport/warrant structure.

== K-CUT-WARRANT precise statement (WarrantClaim.agda)

The warrant component of the keystone, stated as a reviewed theorem
statement with every hypothesis and side condition explicit.  Three
components: grade equality (C1, proven from K-CUT-LOSS and frame
factivity), grade bound (C2, proven with a grade-ordering hypothesis
H4), and data warrant (C3, conditional on a receiver-local side
condition H5).  The gap between type-determined and data-dependent
warrants is documented precisely: the type provides the warrant
structure and type-determined evidence (Kripke knowledge) but not
data-dependent evidence or soundness.

epistemic-types' `SoundWarrant` (pinned commit `495db9cc`) is imported,
not re-declared: `Warrant` carries an `Evidence` type; `SoundWarrant`
adds `sound : Evidence → A`. A sound warrant alone can have an empty
evidence type. The receiver-local side condition therefore also requires
`evidence : Evidence`, so applying `sound` to that token establishes the
data-dependent claim. The evidence type, token and soundness proof are
supplied by the receiver, not derived from the choreography.

What WC-4 says about the keystone: the pre-registration's "SoundWarrant
at the receiver (ASSUMED local soundness, not type-determined)" applies
to data-dependent claims only (C3).  The grade claims (C1, C2) are fully
type-determined: evidence is Kripke knowledge, soundness is frame
factivity, and no external assumption is needed.  The gap is precisely
the boundary between what the choreographic type determines (grades,
projections, bounds) and what it does not (payload properties,
authenticity, cryptographic evidence).  epistemic-types'
`ProofTransport.CertificateCheck` provides the vocabulary for the
receiver-local side condition but does not derive it from the type.

== Grading semantics for bounds under interaction (GradingSemantics.agda)

Defines the grading semantics using the algebraic vocabulary of
tropical-types (Resource/Algebra/Interface.lean, read-only provenance
reference; Dioid.lean, read-only proof witnesses).  Keeps grade,
measure, and index distinct.  Derives one-step bound propagation
theorems and the projection theorem from the declared semantics.
Records a checked negative result for non-commutative ⊗.

tropical-types' Lean 4 proof witnesses (canonicalLe partial order,
monotonicity of both operations, ResourceAlgebra.ofIdempotentAdd) are
provenance, not imports.  The Agda IsOrdered record carries the local
verification.  Bridge obligation M-2 is recorded, not discharged.

[cols="1,3,1",options="header"]
|===
| ID | Claim | Status
| GS-1 | One-step bound propagation for messages: if `grade↾ p G ≼ x`, then `grade↾ p (msg s r g G) ≼ at (role p s r) g ⊗ x`.  Uses ⊗-monotonicity (tropical-types Dioid.lean `canonicalLe_mul_le_mul_left`, provenance; Agda `IsOrdered.⊗-mono`, local verification) | PROVEN (`bound-step-msg`, `bound-step-msg-outsider`)
| GS-2 | One-step bound propagation for choices: if `grade↾ p G₁ ≼ x₁` and `grade↾ p G₂ ≼ x₂`, then `grade↾ p (choice s r g G₁ G₂) ≼ at (role p s r) g ⊗ (x₁ ⊕ x₂)`.  Uses ⊗-monotonicity and ⊕-monotonicity | PROVEN (`bound-step-choice`)
| GS-3 | Two-step bound propagation (composition of GS-1); projection theorem: `restricted-measure p G ≡ local-measure (proj p G)` for projectable types (K-3 restated); bound transfer through projection in both directions | PROVEN (`bound-two-step`, `projection-theorem`, `bound-projection-lower`, `bound-projection-upper`)
| GS-4 | Concrete HWM counterexample: two disjoint interactions have unequal unrestricted measures when followed by end, but equal unrestricted measures when the same interactions are followed by a peak-10, drift-0 continuation. Noncommutativity does not imply that every continuation preserves the difference; a generic unrestricted reordering law also does not follow from the base algebra | PROVEN (`HWMReordering.terminal-order-sensitive`, `continuation-erases-order`)
|===

What GS-4 says about the keystone: tropical-types' Interface.lean does
not assume `mul_comm` in the base `ResourceSemiring`; `CommResourceSemiring`
is an optional refinement. HWM supplies a concrete counterexample to a
uniform unrestricted reordering law. Conversely, commuting grades are
sufficient for equal unrestricted measures under reordering, but equality
after an arbitrary continuation need not imply that the grades commute:
the continuation can erase their difference. `IsSeqMonoid` already has a
right-unit law, which handles the terminal continuation; it is not a
right-cancellation law. Restricted measures remain order-independent for
disjoint interactions (A-1).

== What W-4 says about the keystone

The bound's direction is not a convention: it is the S-3 finding of
resource-algebra (opposite-running orders) surfacing in the keystone. In HWM
and MaxPlus p's view is a *lower* bound on the global grade; in Loss it is
an *upper* bound in ≼, i.e. numerically p's view accounts for no more loss
than the global total. Any K-CUT-WARRANT statement must say which.

== What Y-6 says about the keystone

Synchronously, a message is one event for both of its ends, and T-2's two
halves (receiving is knowing it was received; sending is knowing it was
sent) say the same thing twice. Asynchronously they come apart. The
receiver's knowledge that the message was sent survives (Y-5), because a
receive is causally after its send. The sender's knowledge that it was
received does not (Y-6), and comes back only when something the receiver
did after the receipt reaches the sender (Y-8). The transport component of
K-CUT-WARRANT is therefore directional, and synchrony was what hid the
direction. The loss component is not: Y-2 needs no new law.

== What K-4 says about the keystone

That K-CUT-LOSS survives with only idempotence and a left unit means the
*equality* half of the keystone is algebraically weak: it holds for
non-commutative, non-associative alternatives. The structure the family
cares about — order, monotone sequencing, the opposite-running orders of
Loss and MaxPlus — is first exercised by the *bound* half, K-CUT-WARRANT.
That is where the next proof effort belongs.
