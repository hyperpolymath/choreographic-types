= Pre-registration addendum — 2026-10-19 (ladder increment I3a: asynchrony)
:revdate: 2026-10-19

This addendum fixes the model, the accounting and the claims of the first
I3 increment before any of it is proved. It is committed ahead of the
proofs. Whatever the proofs then show, including any claim that fails, is
recorded against these statements in PROOF-STATUS.adoc, section
"Asynchrony".

== The model, fixed before proving

* *Asynchronous cut.* As before, a cut is a prefix of the global type's
  tree. Now each message, and each choice's selection, inside the prefix is
  either *delivered* or *in flight*: sent, not yet received.
* *Causality.* The receiver of a message in flight does nothing later in
  the prefix. Its own timeline is blocked at the pending receive, and it
  cannot send what it has not yet been told. Nothing else is constrained:
  the sender, and every other party, carries on.
* *Projection.* The sender's local cut includes the send. The receiver's
  local cut stops before the receive. An outsider's is unchanged. Plain
  merge at choices a party is outside of is stated on the parties' views,
  as `Consistent` is for synchronous cuts.
* *Worlds* are causal asynchronous cuts that are consistent for every
  party. As before, two worlds are indistinguishable to a party when its
  view is the same.

== The accounting, fixed before proving

With messages in flight, "the global grade up to the cut" splits into a
sent-so-far grade and a received-so-far grade, and the bound theorems (W-4,
W-5) have to say which they mean. Fixed here:

* *A party's restricted grade.* The sender pays an interaction's grade
  when it sends, and the receiver when it receives. A message in flight has
  been paid for by its sender only.
* *The global grade in the bounds* is the *sent-so-far* grade: the grade of
  the synchronous cut obtained by delivering everything in flight (the
  *completion*). A received-so-far grade would have to drop messages from
  the middle of the prefix, and a dropped middle message leaves no prefix of
  the global type. It is not defined in this increment.

== The falsifier's reading, fixed before proving

The 2026-10-12 addendum said asynchrony should break `sender-knows` and keep
`receiver-knows`. Both T-2 fixtures are instances of "a party knows its own
frontier", and that cannot break: a party's view is its own frontier. The
symmetric statement synchronous K-CUT actually makes is about the *other*
end of a message, and it is read as follows:

* *receiver-knows:* the receiver of a message knows it was sent. Predicted
  to hold asynchronously.
* *sender-knows:* the sender of a message knows it was received. This holds
  synchronously, where a message is one event for both ends. Predicted to
  fail asynchronously at every world where the receiver has done nothing
  since the message, because no reply has come back.

== Claims, each with its falsifier

Y-1:: *Conservativity.* Synchronous cuts are exactly the asynchronous cuts
with nothing in flight. Projection, consistency and the restricted grade
agree with their synchronous counterparts there, and they are causal.
_Falsifier:_ an embedded synchronous cut whose asynchronous projection,
consistency or grade differs.
Y-2:: *Asynchronous K-CUT-LOSS.* For every causal cut consistent for p,
p's restricted grade under the split accounting equals the grade of p's
local view. It is parametric in the resource algebra and uses the same two
laws as K-1 (⊗ left unit, ⊕ idempotence). _Falsifier:_ such a cut where the
two differ, or a proof that needs a third law.
Y-3:: *In-flight invisibility.* Delivering a message in flight changes no
party's view except its receiver's. _Falsifier:_ any other party whose view
changes.
Y-4:: *The asynchronous frame.* "Loss implies warrant" survives (p knows
its own restricted grade), and so does knowledge of the own frontier
(T-2). _Falsifier:_ a world where p's own grade or frontier differs between
two worlds p cannot tell apart.
Y-5:: *Receiver knows sent.* At every world where the first message of a
protocol has been delivered, its receiver knows it was delivered, and so
that it was sent. _Falsifier:_ a receiver-indistinguishable world where it
was not sent.
Y-6:: *Sender does not know receipt.* At every world where the first
message has been delivered but its receiver has done nothing since, the
sender does not know it was received. The same proposition is a theorem in
the synchronous frame. _Falsifier:_ asynchronous knowledge of receipt
without a reply. If that were provable, asynchrony would not separate the
two ends of a message, and the prediction would be refuted.
Y-7:: *Bounds survive with sent-so-far accounting, in both directions.*
If every grade is ≽ 𝟙, p's restricted grade is a known lower bound on the
sent-so-far grade. If every grade is ≼ 𝟙, it is a known upper bound. These
are the W-4 and W-5 analogues; a receiver that lags behind its messages is
the case to watch. _Falsifier:_ a lagging receiver for which the bound
fails.
Y-8:: *A reply restores the knowledge.* In a protocol where the receiver
answers, the sender knows its message was received once the answer has
arrived. _Falsifier:_ a world consistent with the sender's view in which the
answer arrived but the first message was not delivered.

== Scope

* Asynchronous cuts cover the whole fragment, choices included: a
  selection can be in flight. Y-1 to Y-4 and Y-7 are stated for all of it.
* Y-5 and Y-6 are stated for the first message of an arbitrary protocol.
  Deeper messages reduce to that case through delivered prefixes, but the
  general position-indexed statement is not attempted here.
* Y-8 is a single protocol, checked exhaustively over its worlds. The
  general statement, that knowledge of receipt needs a chain of messages
  back, is not attempted here.
* Unchanged: no recursion, no full merge, no delegation. Queues are not
  data: an asynchronous cut records what is in flight, not how it is
  buffered.
